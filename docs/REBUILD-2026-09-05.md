# Shell rebuild, 2026-09-05

Owner's brief: rebuild the site from a blank slate with the clarity of
`jobs.netflix.com` and `lenis.dev`, keep The Spectre's identity, and keep the
five playgrounds. Scope was settled before any code was written:

| Question | Call |
|---|---|
| What gets rebuilt | The whole shell. The five playgrounds and the chat layer port across unchanged. |
| Visual direction | Spectre's own palette and type. The references contribute structure, not appearance. |
| Canonical origin | Undecided. The metadata layer is host-agnostic and switches on one environment variable. |

## What the references actually gave us

Neither site is worth copying. What they share is a grammar:

1. One section holds one idea, announced at poster scale.
2. The palette is two grounds and one accent.
3. The headline is the label. There is no eyebrow above every section.
4. Type carries the page. Ornament does not exist.

Applied here that meant the display scale roughly tripled, the five systems
became five typographic rows instead of a card grid, and the number of
eyebrows above section headlines on the home page went from six to zero.

`lenis.dev`'s numbered feature rows landed on something the house already
owned: DESIGN.md's plate numerals. The roster is `01` to `05` in maroon mono
because that is the brand's device, not because the reference numbered things.

## The one doctrinal change

DESIGN.md's museum logic (white walls, dark rooms) held. The old home page
crossed between the two grounds five times, which reads as oscillation rather
than composition. The page now crosses once, in one direction:

    I    The promise          graphite
    II   The five systems     graphite
    III  One working day      graphite
    IV   The crossing         the dawn photograph, graphite to paper
    V    Proof                paper
    VI   Access               paper

Same two grounds, same doctrine, one deliberate switch. This also satisfies
the "one theme per page" rule that a five-way alternation breaks.

## Second pass, same day

Owner's notes on the first pass: the type was too big and too loud, there was
no scroll choreography, and the page should work harder at showing what
matters. A third note followed: do not number the products.

**Type came down about 40%.** The first pass took the references literally and
set the hero at 128px. Anton at 113px on lenis.dev carries far less optical
weight than a Garamond at the same measurement, because a condensed grotesque
at that size reads as texture and a high-contrast serif reads as a statement.
The hero is now 70px at 1440, section titles 45px. The page gets its presence
from space and from the scroll instead, which is where it should have come
from.

**A scroll engine, `src/components/shell/scroll-scene.tsx`.** One rAF ticker
for the whole site. A scene registers, and each frame the engine writes `--p`
(0 to 1) onto it; CSS does every animation from there through `calc()`. All
rects are read in one pass before any style is written, the loop stops itself
when nothing is near the viewport, and only transform and opacity are ever
animated. No new dependency: it runs on the Lenis instance the site already
had.

Four scenes:

| Scene | What scroll drives |
|---|---|
| Hero | The ridge falls away slower than the page; the statement lifts out of frame. |
| The five systems | Five screens of scroll, pinned. Each screen advances one system. |
| The day | A hairline down the beats draws in step with the reader. The section is about a day passing, so the one moving thing in it is the time. |
| The crossing | The paper ground dissolves in with scroll. You arrive in the dark and leave in the light. |

Plus a page-progress hairline under the navigation bar.

**The pinned systems scene is the centre of the page.** Each system now gets a
full screen carrying its name, its claim, its three concrete capabilities and
its real maturity status. The three capabilities were already in
`products.ts` and were not on the home page at all before this.

**No numbering.** The plate numerals went with the owner's third note, and
what replaced them does more: an index down the left naming all five systems,
marking the one you are on, and jumping the scroll to any of the others. A
numeral said "second of five" and nothing else. The index orients and steers,
and the rule beside the active item doubles as the scene's progress bar.

The same reasoning removed the `01 02 03` above the three capability rows on
each product page and above the four principles on the method page: those sets
have no order, and numbering them asserts one. They carry an accent rule now,
in the page's own line language. The notes index lost its numerals too, since
it was the last counted list left.

**What it degrades to.** Below 900px, and under `prefers-reduced-motion`, no
scene registers. The stage collapses to its natural height, the frame stops
sticking, and all five systems sit in the flow as a stacked list with every
fact still on it. `--p` stays 0, and every rule is written so that 0 means
finished rather than not started. The only thing lost is the choreography.

## Third pass

Owner's notes: the crossing's type is too small, and the page should have the
kind of scroll work lenis.dev has.

**Looked at what lenis.dev actually does** rather than guessing. Its home page
is a fixed WebGL canvas, a sticky hero, and a sticky stack of seven feature
cards driven by `--progress`, `--index` and `--i` custom properties. That last
part is the same architecture built here, which was reassuring. What it has
that this page did not is **scale contrast** and **a second axis**.

**Scale contrast.** The second pass brought everything down together, and that
was half right: the running headlines were shouting, but flattening the
manifesto to the size of a section title threw away the contrast that makes a
page feel composed. A site should have one voice louder than the rest. Here it
is the sentence the company is built on, so `.display-manifesto` exists, is
used exactly once, and runs to 96px at 1440. Everything else stays quiet so
that can be loud. The three guarantees under it moved out of the 11px stamp
register, where they read as a legal footnote under the claim they are meant
to be backing, and into the text face at 16px.

**The manifesto lights word by word as you scroll it.** The whole effect is
one declaration:

    opacity: calc(0.2 + ((var(--p) - 0.3) * 4) * var(--n) - var(--i))

`--n` is the word count, `--i` the word's place, `--p` the scene's progress.
Each word's opacity is its own distance behind the advancing front, and the
browser clamps to 0 and 1 for free. No JavaScript per word. The timing is set
against the section's real geometry: progress reaches about 0.39 as the block
locks to the top of the viewport and about 0.61 as it releases, so the
lighting runs 0.30 to 0.53 and the last word lands while the block is still
held. The paper ground was originally washing in at 0.42, underneath words
that had not lit yet; it now waits until 0.56.

**A second axis: the day pans sideways.** Scrolling down moves one working
Tuesday left to right through its hours, which is the axis a day actually
has, and it is a different device from the pinned scene above it so the page
does not repeat itself. The travel is one line of CSS:

    transform: translateX(calc(var(--p) * (100vw - 100%)))

A percentage in `translateX` resolves against the element's own width, so at
progress 1 the track has moved exactly far enough to bring its right edge to
the right edge of the screen, whatever the four panels add up to. Nothing is
measured in JavaScript and nothing breaks when the copy changes length.

Below 1000px and under reduced motion the pan never registers and the four
beats become a vertical list, because a horizontal pan on a phone fights the
reader's thumb. The approval still works there, which is the point of the
section.

## Fourth pass: the art

Owner's note: the backgrounds feel bland. Go and look at how the sites in the
Lenis showcase handle art.

**What I found.** I probed a handful of them for what actually makes the
grounds feel like something. Studio Freight runs a near-white ground with
`mix-blend-mode: difference` and an inverting overlay. Properly Studio is
pure black with 360 images, eight videos, and 36 to 70px blurs used as bloom.
Carre Studio is flat but carries the whole page on type. lenis.dev itself
runs a fixed WebGL canvas behind everything.

None of that transfers directly. Three of the four buy their richness with
photography this brand does not have: DESIGN.md §7 sanctions exactly one
photograph plus the crossing plate, and any new one needs the owner's call.
So the answer had to come from what the house already owns.

**Four changes.**

*The grounds have a light source.* Flat fill over four screens of graphite
was most of the problem. Every ground now carries a single soft falloff from
a high point, two stops, no animation and no second hue. This is light, not
a gradient effect, and it is DESIGN.md §9b's approved ambient sheen applied
as static light rather than as a nine-second breathe.

*The page has a surface.* A fine tooth over everything at three percent
opacity, fixed and never animated, so it composites once. The brand's whole
metaphor is paper and plates, and a flat RGB fill has no tooth. At this alpha
it is not visible as texture, only felt as material. **This is the one thing
in the pass that reads against a strict take on §7's ban on decorative
noise, and it is the one Codex should rule on.**

*The motif became the art.* §6 gives each system a schematic line-texture and
says it may run as a hero backdrop, self-drawing. It was doing neither: 360px
in a corner at 42%, static. It now fills the right third of the pinned scene
at true hairline weight and is wiped in diagonally as the reader moves
through that system. Two details made it work: `vector-effect:
non-scaling-stroke`, without which a 90-unit schematic blown up to 420px
draws its hairlines at seven pixels and stops being a schematic; and a mask
gradient rather than a stroke-dash draw, because `pathLength` is uneven
across browsers on circles and rects and half these schematics are built from
them.

*The scene has a horizon.* The ridge returns behind the pinned systems,
graded to `brightness(0.62)` and washed so the left two thirds stay a solid
ground and the right opens onto the photograph. A picture behind body text is
a contrast problem; a picture beside it is depth. The file is already in
cache by the time the reader arrives.

**The paper half stays quiet on purpose.** Proof and access are where a reader
reads and acts, so their emphasis comes from type rather than from more art:
the three evidence figures went up to 4.2rem, which is what §7 means by
setting numbers as instrument readouts.

Contrast was re-checked against the new lit extremes of both grounds. Worst
pair is 5.85:1.

## Fifth pass: the ledger

Owner's note: the motif work was not creativity, absorb what Lenis is
actually about, and make this award-category.

**What Lenis is actually about.** Its own copy says smooth scroll "pulls
users into the flow of the experience that feels so substantial that they
forget they're navigating a web page." The idea is not the easing. It is
that scroll stops being navigation and becomes a single continuous medium,
one clock driving one world, and that the interaction carries the argument
rather than decorating it.

Measured against that, everything up to this pass was decoration. The site
had good scroll work and it was still *describing* the approval gate in
sentences. A reader can agree with a sentence and feel nothing.

**So the page stops saying it and starts doing it.**

A rail follows the reader down the entire page. As they read, the system
works: their context is read, memory is indexed, an order is tested against
live capacity, a week is drafted, claims are sourced and challenged, reviews
are composed, fourteen items are prepared. Each line lands with a gold point,
which in this house means one thing and one thing only. The counter climbs.

And nothing happens. The reader scrolls past five systems and a whole working
day and the rail just keeps staging. No send, no publish, no spend, because
there is no code path that goes around the gate.

Then one control. They tap it, the batch releases at once, every line takes a
timestamp, the gold goes out, and the rail reads `Released by you at 14:37.
Logged.`

The reader has just operated the product without being told they were going
to. The argument lands as a memory of having done something rather than as a
paragraph they agreed with. That is what the site was missing, and no amount
of background art was going to supply it.

**Design decisions worth keeping.**

- *It is closed the whole way down.* One line: a gold point, `STAGED`, and a
  count. A device arguing for restraint has no business shouting. It opens
  itself exactly once, on the desktop, at the moment the batch completes and
  it stops being a tally and becomes a decision. On a phone it never opens
  itself, because there it spans the screen.
- *It is a dark plate on both grounds.* It is fixed, and the page crosses
  from graphite into paper underneath it, so it cannot match its background.
  It can only decide what it is. An instrument reads as an instrument on a
  desk of any colour.
- *The gate arms on the batch, not on a scroll position.* An approval exists
  because there is a finished batch to approve. That is also what makes it
  hold on a phone, where the pinned scene never runs.
- *The day stopped carrying its own approve button.* There is now exactly one
  approval on the page and it is the reader's.
- *It is stamped.* `SCRIPTED DEMONSTRATION. NOTHING LEAVES THIS BROWSER.`
  Nothing is computed and nothing leaves the page. A device this persuasive
  is precisely the wrong place to get loose about the house rule.

**A bug this surfaced.** The pinned scene announced its first system on
mount, so the ledger staged work before the reader had seen the hero.
`useSteppedScene` now reports whether the reader has actually entered the
scene, and the announcement waits for that. The engine only measures a scene
that is near the viewport, so the first tick is the proof.

## Sixth pass: colour

Owner's note, after seeing the fifth: still dark, bland and boring. New
references, `sports.withgoogle.com/teamusa` and `annnimate.com`.

**What those actually are.** Both were probed rather than guessed at.

| Site | Ground | Where the life comes from |
|---|---|---|
| lenis.dev | black | WebGL canvas, 113px condensed type |
| Team USA | **white** | H2 at **210px**, 20 videos, one saturated blue |
| annnimate | dark | a hot orange on **299 elements as a field** |

Every one of them gets its energy from saturated colour used as a field and
type at two to three times our scale. Which means the thing making this site
bland was not a missing texture. It was `DESIGN.md`.

**The rules being broken, deliberately.**

- §1: *"the maroon is an accent, never a fill. Across large areas it reads
  heavy and cheapens the effect."*
- §2 rule 2: *"HOME is near-mono. The five hues appear only as 8px identity
  dots."*
- §1: *"Hairlines, not boxes."*

The first is true of a flat mid-maroon behind marketing copy. It is not true
of a deep oxblood carrying one sentence at 8.5rem and nothing else, which is
the oldest trick in print and the reason a Penguin spine looks expensive. The
second is the rule that cost the most: five hues were defined as a considered
band and then spent on 8px dots.

**What the page is now.** A colour journey rather than a document:

    I    Hero          the ridge, lifted out of murk, type at 118px
    II   Five systems  five full-bleed grounds, one per system
    III  One day       bright paper, panning sideways
    IV   The crossing  a deep maroon room, manifesto at 8.5rem
    V    Proof         paper
    VI   Access        paper

The centrepiece is II. Each system owns the whole screen while you are inside
it, and scrolling moves you through all five: spectral, steel, clay, archive,
ochre, deepened from their text variants because a field wants more body than
a hairline does. The schematic goes to 620px in paper on top of its own
ground, where it is structure rather than ornament.

What survives from §2 is the rule that actually mattered: **one hue is on
screen at a time, ever.** The five stay a single band of desaturated jewel
tones rather than a rainbow, and the transition between them is slow enough
to read as a room changing rather than a theme switching.

**Contrast was the constraint, not an afterthought.** Every field was tested
for paper text and for the 72% ghost on top of it. The ochre failed at
4.26:1, so the value was solved for rather than eyeballed and deepened to
`#634f24`. Worst pair across all six grounds is now 4.61:1.

| Ground | paper | ghost |
|---|---|---|
| PA spectral `#4b466f` | 7.95 | 5.03 |
| COO steel `#2f4c73` | 7.96 | 5.03 |
| CMO clay `#7c3826` | 7.81 | 4.88 |
| Researcher archive `#3c5844` | 7.16 | 4.61 |
| HR ochre `#634f24` | 7.15 | 4.63 |
| Maroon room `#5a171b` | 12.16 | 6.92 |

**This needs the owner's ruling.** Not because it is wrong, but because it is
a brand decision and not a styling one. `DESIGN.md` §1 and §2 no longer
describe the site, and one of the two documents has to change.

## Seventh pass: coherence, and the brand book

Owner: "you do what's best, change what's best." Two things were clearly
best.

**The five systems owned a colour on the home page and then ignored it on
their own page.** That was the largest remaining incoherence on the site: a
reader who scrolled through the clay room and clicked into AI CMO arrived
somewhere that looked like a different company. Every product page now opens
full bleed on the ground its system owns, with its schematic at 620px behind
the type, and cuts to paper for the demo, the claims and the form. The colour
is the arrival, not the whole page, because a demo frame and a form both want
a quiet ground.

The navigation bar was unreadable over those grounds. Rather than listing the
pages that need a light bar, it now detects whether the page opens on one
(`main .hero, main .product-open`), so a new page that opens the same way is
handled without anyone remembering.

**`DESIGN.md` no longer described this site,** and a brand book that
contradicts the product is worse than no brand book. It has been revised
rather than annotated:

- §1 rewritten. Maroon is a field as well as a rule, the grounds have a light
  source, gold is still a state and still non-negotiable, surfaces are still
  unused.
- §2 rewritten. Five grounds, not five dots, with the field and deep values
  tabled and the contrast work recorded. The rule that survived is the one
  that mattered: one hue on screen at a time, ever.
- §5 rewritten to describe the scroll engine and its five scenes.
- §6 rewritten: the schematic is structure on its own ground, not a 20%
  watermark in a corner.
- §10 replaced. It used to ask for a verdict on radius and brass; it now asks
  for one on the reversal itself, on the surface grain, and on the ledger.

The three things left for the owner are named there rather than buried here.

## Motion

One signature in two forms. Blocks rise 14px. Display headlines reveal a word
at a time out of a clipped line, 45ms apart.

Both are additive. The server renders finished markup; the hiding class is
applied on the client, before paint, and only when motion is allowed. A reader
without JavaScript and a crawler both get the whole page. Getting this the
wrong way round hides the most important text on the page from the people
least able to recover it, and it was wrong in the first draft of this rebuild
until a browser check caught it.

Under `prefers-reduced-motion` everything resolves instantly, the word clip is
released, and the approval control still works.

## SEO

- One `h1` per page, unique title and description on all nine routes.
- `rel=canonical` on every page, derived from one function.
- `sitemap.xml` and `robots.txt` generated at build.
- JSON-LD: Organization and WebSite once on the home page, a Service per
  product referencing the organisation by `@id`, BreadcrumbList on every
  sub-page, Article on each note.
- The hero photograph is preloaded with `fetchPriority="high"`; it is the LCP
  element and this is a static export, so nothing else tells the browser
  about it before the stylesheet resolves.

### The open item

`thespectre.one` and `k-crypt.github.io/spectre` are both serving this site.
That is duplicate content, and the previous build hard-coded `metadataBase` to
the Netlify host regardless of where it deployed.

Nothing here picks a winner, because that is the owner's call. Instead:

- `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL` decides the canonical origin for every canonical tag,
  OG url, sitemap entry and JSON-LD id.
- Absent that variable, a GitHub Pages build derives its own origin and ships
  `noindex` plus a `Disallow: /` robots file, so the two origins cannot
  compete while the decision is open.
- Setting the variable on whichever build should win flips all of it at once.
